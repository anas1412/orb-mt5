Passing a 5 / 10 account

A different rule set to the one the other pages model: 5% daily, 10% maximum loss, +12% target, at 2% a trade. Two strategies, one in the Asia session and one in New York, so at most two trades a day — 4% if both lose, with 1% of headroom against the daily limit. Everything below is resampled from the 145 days those two really produced in 2026.

Two trades at 2% cannot breach a 5% daily limit — the worst possible day is 4%, leaving 1% of room. The daily limit is not what kills this account; the 10% maximum loss is, and it takes a run of losing days rather than a run of losing trades.

On the real 2026 sequence the account's worst peak-to-trough was 8.1 R — -16.3% at 2% — from 29 Jun to 16 Jul. That is the number a trailing limit measures, and it would have breached this one.

But a static 10% limit is measured from the starting balance, and equity never once dipped below it — running the whole of 2026 from 1 January, the account was never behind. The distinction is the whole difference between passing and not: once the account is up, a giveback that looks alarming is nowhere near the line.

Both single-strategy runs above cost more than 10% at 2% — the ORB's worst six in a row is -12.2%. Those runs did not end an attempt because they did not arrive at the start of one. A losing run in the first fortnight is fatal; the same run after +8% is survivable. That is what the pass rate is really measuring.

08What this does not show

  • 2026 only. Every path is resampled from the same 145 days. A simulation cannot invent a market the data never contained, so the pass rate is what these two strategies would do in a year like this one, not in any year.
  • Resampling breaks the order. Real losing runs cluster more than independent draws do, which makes the drawdown figures optimistic. The measured runs in section 03 are the honest counterweight — those are what actually happened, in sequence.
  • The NQ fade's parameters were chosen on this data, and its expectancy on 2024 and 2025 is about a fifth of what it shows here. Halve its contribution and the pass rate falls.
  • No slippage beyond the spread, no missed fills, no news halt, no execution error. Every trade is taken.
  • One account. Running several at once correlates them perfectly — the same trades on each — so it multiplies the outcome rather than diversifying it.
