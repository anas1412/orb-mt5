Fading yesterday's high and low

Price sweeps yesterday’s extreme, fails to hold, and an M5 candle closes back inside. You take that close and trade against the sweep. Gold, Tuesday to Friday, entries in the Asia session, 2.5 R target, held to the end of the day. 2026 only — 54 trades.

01The rules

Two numbers off yesterday's chart, then one candle to wait for.

  1. Mark yesterday's high and lowThose are the two levels. Subtract them for yesterday's range — you need it for the stop.
  2. Tuesday to Friday onlyMonday is skipped: its levels come from Friday with a weekend in between, so the high and low being faded are three days stale. Monday returned −0.050 R a trade against +0.527 for the other four days.
  3. Take entries only between 00:00 and 08:00 UTCThe Asia session. Entering during London or New York loses money on these same rules — section 05. The exit is not restricted to Asia; see rule 9.
  4. Wait for a sweepPrice has to trade beyond one of the levels. Above yesterday's high, or below yesterday's low.
  5. Wait for an M5 candle to CLOSE back insideThat close is the signal the sweep failed. A wick back inside does not count.
  6. Skip it if the close ran more than 600 points back insideBy then the snap-back has already happened and you are late. It also caps how large your stop can get.
  7. Enter at market on that closeSell if the high was swept, buy if the low was. No limit order, no waiting for a retest.
  8. Stop: yesterday's range × 0.25, beyond the levelMeasured from the level, not from your entry — so the sweep itself cannot take you out.
  9. Target: 2.5 × your riskRisk is entry to stop, which is a little more than 0.25 × the range because you entered inside the level. A bigger target is deliberate: on a challenge account you need a lump, and three wins of this size clear a 12% goal where three smaller ones do not.
  10. Close it before the day ends — never carry it overnightNot the end of Asia: the position runs on through London and New York and is flat by the broker day’s close. The median trade lasts 632 minutes and the latest exit in 2026 was 23:49 broker time. Cutting the hold at 08:00 UTC instead drops the result from +32.7 R to +20.5 R, so most of the move arrives long after the entry.

A worked example

Yesterday: high 4650.00, low 4548.00 → range 10200 points, × 0.25 = 2550 points.

Price pokes above 4650, then an M5 candle closes back below it at 4646.00 — 400 points back inside, under the 600 limit, so it is valid.

Sell 4646.00 · stop 4650 + 2550 = 4675.50 · risk 2950 points · target 4572.25  (2.5 × 2950)

06What is weak about this

Everything below is a reason not to size this like a proven edge.

Small sample, and it was searched

54 trades over nine months. The configuration was picked from a grid of roughly a thousand. t = 2.78 — it clears the usual bar, but the usual bar assumes one test, not a thousand.

2026 only, and 2024–25 lose

These same rules lose money on 2024 and 2025. That may be regime — gold's Asia range tripled into 2026 — or it may be that 2026 is the year the noise happened to line up.

The level itself may not matter

A placebo test replaced yesterday's high and low with the high and low from ten days ago, and the search scored just as well. What is being paid for is waiting after an overshoot, not yesterday's orders specifically.

A day trade, but a slow one

Nothing is carried overnight: every one of the 65 trades closes inside the same broker day, the latest at 23:49 broker time. But the hold is long — median 632 minutes, and the target takes a median of 728 minutes to arrive. 13 of 54 exit after 22:00 broker time, so depending on where your broker sets its rollover some of them may be charged one swap. Spread is charged at entry; swap is not modelled.

The Monday filter rests on an argument, not on significance

Monday is excluded because its levels come from Friday with a weekend in between — a mechanism, not a pattern spotted in the data. The measurement agrees but cannot carry the claim on its own: 11 Monday trades at -0.147 R against +0.606 R for Tuesday to Friday, and shuffling the trades between the two groups beats that gap about a quarter of the time. Four other weekdays were looked at, so the worst of five looking bad is expected.

The 600-point skip was chosen after the fact

It survives a constant-risk control (so it is not just arithmetic) and sits at the 98.3rd percentile of dropping 17 trades at random — but seven thresholds were tried, and correcting for that leaves it merely suggestive. Treat it as risk control with a possible bonus.

One month carries more than its share

Delete the single best month (Apr) and the rest returns +0.473 R a trade against +0.606 R with it — the result survives, but it thins. On 65 trades that is the most useful robustness check available, and it is a check, not a result.

What does hold up

Entering the moment price touches the level loses badly. Waiting for the M5 close back inside is worth roughly +0.27 R per trade, and that part is stable across every cut tested.
