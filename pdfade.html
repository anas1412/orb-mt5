Fading yesterday's high and low

Price sweeps yesterday’s extreme, fails to hold, and an M5 candle closes back inside. You take that close and trade against the sweep. Gold, entries in the Asia session, held to the end of the day. 2026 only — 65 trades.

%

Every percentage on this page is R × risk. The R figures, the win rate and the payoff do not move. The worst losing run on record is 3.0 R and the worst drawdown 3.2 R, so a 12% maximum loss is not reached until 3.69% and one losing trade breaches a 3% daily limit past 3.00%.

01The rules

Two numbers off yesterday's chart, then one candle to wait for.

  1. Mark yesterday's high and lowThose are the two levels. Subtract them for yesterday's range — you need it for the stop.
  2. Take entries only between 00:00 and 08:00 UTCThe Asia session. Entering during London or New York loses money on these same rules — section 05. The exit is not restricted to Asia; see rule 9.
  3. Wait for a sweepPrice has to trade beyond one of the levels. Above yesterday's high, or below yesterday's low.
  4. Wait for an M5 candle to CLOSE back insideThat close is the signal the sweep failed. A wick back inside does not count.
  5. Skip it if the close ran more than 600 points back insideBy then the snap-back has already happened and you are late. It also caps how large your stop can get.
  6. Enter at market on that closeSell if the high was swept, buy if the low was. No limit order, no waiting for a retest.
  7. Stop: yesterday's range ÷ 3, beyond the levelMeasured from the level, not from your entry — so the sweep itself cannot take you out.
  8. Target: 2 × your riskRisk is entry to stop, which is a little more than range ÷ 3 because you entered inside the level.
  9. Hold to the end of the trading dayNot the end of Asia. If neither the stop nor the target is hit by the broker day’s close, take whatever it is worth. The median trade runs 799 minutes — through London and New York. Cutting the hold at 08:00 UTC drops the result from +28.4 R to +16.4 R, so most of the move arrives long after the entry.

A worked example

Yesterday: high 4650.00, low 4548.00 → range 10200 points, ÷3 = 3400 points.

Price pokes above 4650, then an M5 candle closes back below it at 4646.00 — 400 points back inside, under the 600 limit, so it is valid.

Sell 4646.00 · stop 4650 + 3400 = 4684.00 · risk 3800 points · target 4646 − 7600 = 4570.00

05What is weak about this

Everything below is a reason not to size this like a proven edge.

Small sample, and it was searched

65 trades over nine months. The configuration was picked from a grid of roughly a thousand. t = 2.69 — it clears the usual bar, but the usual bar assumes one test, not a thousand.

2026 only, and 2024–25 lose

These same rules lose money on 2024 and 2025. That may be regime — gold's Asia range tripled into 2026 — or it may be that 2026 is the year the noise happened to line up.

The level itself may not matter

A placebo test replaced yesterday's high and low with the high and low from ten days ago, and the search scored just as well. What is being paid for is waiting after an overshoot, not yesterday's orders specifically.

It is an overnight trade, and financing is not modelled

Median hold 799 minutes; the target takes a median of 733 minutes to arrive and 19 trades ran to the day’s close. Spread is charged at entry, but swap and any rollover crossing are not. A position carried that long on gold has a real financing cost this study does not subtract.

The 600-point skip was chosen after the fact

It survives a constant-risk control (so it is not just arithmetic) and sits at the 98.3rd percentile of dropping 17 trades at random — but seven thresholds were tried, and correcting for that leaves it merely suggestive. Treat it as risk control with a possible bonus.

What does hold up

Entering the moment price touches the level loses badly. Waiting for the M5 close back inside is worth roughly +0.27 R per trade, and that part is stable across every cut tested.
