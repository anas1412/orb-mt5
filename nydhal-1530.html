01The rules

Everything is mechanical. No discretion, no judgement calls, and nothing that needs you awake at a screen.

  1. Mark the rangeTake the highest high and lowest low of the fifteen M1 candles from 13:30 to 13:44 UTC. That box is the range.
  2. Split the box in half and see where the 13:44 candle closedClosed in the top half → you may only take an upward break. Closed in the bottom half → only a downward break. Breaks the other way are skipped — section 02.
  3. Watch for a break until 00:29The first M1 candle to close outside the box triggers the trade — above the high goes long, below the low goes short. Wicks poking through don't count. Nothing by 00:29 means no trade.
  4. Enter at marketOn the next candle, in the direction of the break.
  5. Stop at the range midpointHalfway between the high and the low. That distance is your 1R.
  6. Target at 1.2R1.2× the distance from entry to stop, measured from the actual fill.
  7. The stop never movesSet once at the range midpoint and left there. Every loss is a full 1R.
  8. Flat after 60 minutesIf neither level is hit, close at market. 46% of trades finish on their own before this.
  9. Monday to Friday, one trade a dayOne position, then done regardless of the outcome.
  10. Risk 2.5% of the starting balanceFixed, not compounding. Position size = 2.5% ÷ the distance from entry to stop.

03Why each rule exists

Every number was measured, not chosen. Here is the reasoning in plain terms.

RuleWhy this and not something else
15:30 UTC+2 (13:30 UTC)Nydhal's choice, not a search result. It is the same session as the existing 13:30 study, so the two are directly comparable.
One M15 candle as the box15:30 to 15:44. The high and low of that single candle are the whole range — no 15 separate M1 candles to aggregate.
M15 close to confirm the breakA wick through the box does not count. Roughly half of all breaks happen on the very first M15 candle after the box.
Entries until 17:00 UTC+2Five M15 candles. His rule 4. Breaks later than that were not tested, because the bar data runs out before a 60-minute hold would finish.
Stop at the far side of the boxThe one change from his spec. His rule 5 puts it at the break candle's low, which measured +0.025 R a trade against +0.074 R here. The far-side stop is roughly $22 wide against $14, and a fixed spread-and-commission is a smaller share of the bigger number: 0.031 R against 0.052 R.
Not the candle before the breakHis rule 6. Tested at every target and it never beat the box edge. When the break lands on the first candle it is the box edge anyway.
Market entry, on the candle's closeThe order goes in the moment the M15 candle closes beyond the box; the fill is the next minute's open. The alternative — a limit resting back at the box edge — only fills 73% of the time and earns +0.053 R against +0.088 R, because the trades it misses are the ones that ran away.
1.2R targetHe asked for 1R, which earns +0.095 R a trade. 1.2R earns +0.118 R for the same worst losing run, so the extra 0.2R is free. 0.5R is the worst choice of all — a smaller payout meets the same fixed cost. Everything from 1.1R to 1.3R scores between +0.099 and +0.118, so read 1.2R as the middle of a plateau rather than a precise peak.
No stop moveA 1R target is reached or not before a stop move at +0.5R would earn its keep.
60-minute capNot in his spec, which runs to stop or target. It is here for drawdown, not for profit: running to resolution earns the same +0.093 R a trade and a better 55.4% win rate, but the worst losing run doubles from -3.02 R to -5.03 R, which at 2.5% risk is 12.6% and breaches the 12% limit on its own. The cap turns would-be full stops into small exits. It is also the honest limit of the data — 32.5% of no-cap trades are closed by the bar file ending, not by the rule.
Either directionHis rules 1 and 2: the first M15 candle to close outside the box is taken whichever way it broke. 83 longs, 74 shorts.
2026 only — read thisThe same rules over 2024-2026 lose money on all 32 stop-and-target settings tested. Gold is far more expensive in 2026, so the box is wider in dollars and a fixed cost matters less. That is the whole reason this year looks different, and it is the main reason to distrust the numbers below.
Evidence strength — and thisThe best t-statistic across every variant is about +1.3. Anything under +2 is indistinguishable from luck. On this many trades the result is a hint, not a finding.

The one risk that matters

A 5-loss run happened once, and at 2.5% risk it costs 12.5% — past the 12% limit.

Realised drawdown reached 12.7% because a winner landed before that run started from a peak. The same sequence hitting a fresh account is the failure case, and it is the only one worth planning for — nearly every simulated failure is one long losing run rather than a slow bleed.

Position sizing, worked

  • Account $10,000, risk 2.5% → $250 per trade, every trade.
  • Range is 1,000 points ($10.00) → stop sits 500 points ($5.00) away.
  • Gold moves $1 per point per lot, so $5.00 of stop = $500 per lot.
  • Size = $250 ÷ $500 = 0.50 lots.

A wider range means a wider stop and therefore a smaller position — the dollar risk stays at $200 regardless. That is what makes every result comparable in R.
