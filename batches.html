01Who takes what

The rotation is by signal, not by weekday, so a pair waits for its turn rather than for a particular day — and the weekday each pair lands on drifts by itself.

Four accounts, two outcomes

A1 and A2 take the same signal at the same moment, so they pass or fail together. A batch of four is two distinct attempts held twice — two fees per attempt. Starting the second account of each pair a week later would give four different outcomes at the same speed.

03Room at 2.5%

Risk is capped at 2.5% per trade. The question is whether that keeps a single bad trade clear of the 3% daily limit, because one trade past it ends the account on its own.

At 2.5% per trade a loss of 1.20 R is the whole daily limit.

The A batch has little room, and it is measured room

The opening range fills past its stop on 24 of 75 trades — real-tick execution, spread and slippage included — worst case -1.094 R. At 2.5% that costs 2.74% of the 3.00% daily limit, leaving 0.26%. A fill only 10% worse than anything on record would breach the day on one trade.

The B batch's larger headroom is not real

Every fade stop fills at exactly −1.000 R, because that study replays M1 bars and takes the stop at its level — it models no slippage past the stop at all. Its 0.50% is therefore optimistic; give it the overshoot the A batch actually shows and it lands where the A batch does.

What the rotation buys

It is not throughput — each account is slower, because it waits out every other signal. It is that the two pairs hold different trades, so no single session can take the whole batch: all four fail together only 3.5% of the time in batch A and 13.9% in batch B.
