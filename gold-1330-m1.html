01The rules

Everything is mechanical. No discretion, no judgement calls, and nothing that needs you awake at a screen.

  1. Mark the rangeTake the highest high and lowest low of the fifteen M1 candles from 13:30 to 13:44 UTC. That box is the range.
  2. Split the box in half and see where the 13:44 candle closedClosed in the top half → you may only take an upward break. Closed in the bottom half → only a downward break. Breaks the other way are skipped — section 02.
  3. Watch for a break until 00:29The first M1 candle to close outside the box triggers the trade — above the high goes long, below the low goes short. Wicks poking through don't count. Nothing by 00:29 means no trade.
  4. Enter at marketOn the next candle, in the direction of the break.
  5. Stop at the range midpointHalfway between the high and the low. That distance is your 1R.
  6. Target at 2RTwice the distance from entry to stop, measured from the actual fill.
  7. At +0.5R, move the stop to −0.5ROnce the trade is halfway to target, pull the stop so the worst case is half a loss instead of a full one. Once only — it never moves again.
  8. Flat after 90 minutesIf neither level is hit, close at market. 95% of trades finish on their own before this.
  9. Monday to Thursday, one trade a dayFridays off. One position, then done regardless of the outcome.
  10. Risk 2.5% of the starting balanceFixed, not compounding. Position size = 2.5% ÷ the distance from entry to stop.

02The half-of-the-range rule

One filter, and it is the only one the edge needs. Draw a line across the middle of the range box and ask which half the 13:44 candle closed in. That half is the only direction you are allowed to trade today.

The whole rule

Closed in the top half → longs only. If price breaks down instead, you sit out.
Closed in the bottom half → shorts only. If price breaks up instead, you sit out.

Reading it live, in three seconds

  • At 13:45, look at the box and find its middle.
  • Did the last candle close above the middle? Only a break of the high counts today.
  • Below the middle? Only a break of the low counts.
  • Price breaks the wrong side → no trade, close the platform.
The one thing people get wrong

The candle's colour doesn't matter. A red candle sitting near the top of the box still counts as the top half — it drifted down a little but price is still up there. You are reading where it is, not which way it moved.

Every 2026 Monday–Thursday break, split by which half the range closed in. Totals, not averages, except EV. Total % is the account move at 2.5% risk per trade. Profit factor is R won divided by R lost — 2.00 means twice as much won as lost.

Why this filter is worth having

Trading every break returns -18.0 R from 107 trades at a 23.4% win rate. Trading only the correct half returns -12.5 R from 76 — virtually the same money from 31 fewer trades, at a 22.4 win rate. The 31 skipped trades contributed -5.4 R between them: all of the drawdown, none of the profit. Fewer trades at a higher win rate is exactly what a pass-rate account wants.

The one risk that matters

A 9-loss run happened 2 times, and at 2.5% risk it costs 22.5% — past the 12% limit.

Realised drawdown reached 36.4% because a winner landed before that run started from a peak. The same sequence hitting a fresh account is the failure case, and it is the only one worth planning for — nearly every simulated failure is one long losing run rather than a slow bleed.

Position sizing, worked

  • Account $10,000, risk 2.5% → $250 per trade, every trade.
  • Range is 1,000 points ($10.00) → stop sits 500 points ($5.00) away.
  • Gold moves $1 per point per lot, so $5.00 of stop = $500 per lot.
  • Size = $250 ÷ $500 = 0.50 lots.

A wider range means a wider stop and therefore a smaller position — the dollar risk stays at $200 regardless. That is what makes every result comparable in R.
