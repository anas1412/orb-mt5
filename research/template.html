01The rules

Everything is mechanical. No discretion, no judgement calls, and nothing that needs you awake at a screen.

  1. Mark the rangeTake the highest high and lowest low of the {{RANGEMIN}} M1 candles from {{RANGETXT}}. That box is the range.
  2. Split the box in half and see where the {{LASTCANDLE}} candle closedClosed in the top half → you may only take an upward break. Closed in the bottom half → only a downward break. Breaks the other way are skipped — section 02.
  3. Watch for a break until 00:29The first M1 candle to close outside the box triggers the trade — above the high goes long, below the low goes short. Wicks poking through don't count. Nothing by 00:29 means no trade.
  4. Enter at marketOn the next candle, in the direction of the break.
  5. Stop at the range midpointHalfway between the high and the low. That distance is your 1R.
  6. Target at {{RRTXT}}{{RRWORD}} the distance from entry to stop, measured from the actual fill.
    7. {{STOPRULE}}
  7. Flat after {{HOLDMIN}} minutesIf neither level is hit, close at market. {{SELFPCT}} of trades finish on their own before this.
  8. {{DAYSTXT}}, one trade a day{{OFFDAYS}}One position, then done regardless of the outcome.
  9. Risk of the starting balanceFixed, not compounding. Position size = ÷ the distance from entry to stop.

Position sizing, worked

  • Account {{DEPOSIT}}, risk → per trade, every trade.
  • Range is 1,000 points ($10.00) → stop sits 500 points ($5.00) away.
  • Gold moves $1 per point per lot, so $5.00 of stop = $500 per lot.
  • Size = ÷ $500 = lots.

A wider range means a wider stop and therefore a smaller position — the dollar risk stays at $200 regardless. That is what makes every result comparable in R.
