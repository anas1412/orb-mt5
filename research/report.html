Parameter Study · XAUUSD Asia Session

Opening Range Breakout

546 configurations · 481 setups · 2024.01.01 – 2026.08.19 · FundingPips 2-Step Standard

Three questions were asked: where to place the stop, what reward-to-risk to target, and when and where to move the stop. All three have clear answers. A fourth finding, not asked for, matters more than any of them.

Verdict

The configuration already running is the optimum. Stop at the range midpoint, target 2R, stop moved to −0.5R once the trade reaches +0.5R. Out of 546 candidates it ranks first on pass rate to within a tenth of a point, and no change is warranted.

But that optimum only exists in 2026. Not one of the 546 configurations is profitable in 2024 or 2025 — the best "worst year" in the entire grid is −0.042R per trade.

00The regime problem

Gold's realised volatility ran above 50% in 2026 against a long-run norm near 15–16%. The 15-minute Asia range widened from $3.01 to $18.73 — six times in dollars, and 3.3× even after adjusting for the higher price.

Basis for selection

Parameters below are chosen on 2026 data only, on the reasoning that Asia-session participation in gold changed in scale and 2024–25 describes a market that no longer exists. That is defensible. The cost is 119 setups and no out-of-sample test remaining — forward live results are the only validation left. The 2024–25 columns are kept as the answer to "what if the regime reverts", and that answer is about −0.1R per trade at every setting.

Answer: the midpoint, 0.5. Best for 2026 by a wide margin — +0.362 against +0.138 for the far side.

Tight stops are catastrophic, and monotonically so. A quarter of the range loses 0.272R per trade. The stop sits inside normal noise, so it gets taken out before the move resolves: you pay full risk on a coin that never lands. Everything below 0.5 is worse in every year. This is the most robust single finding in the study.

The far side is the defensive choice. Best overall EV (−0.004) and highest win rate (39.1%), because the stop is far enough away that fewer trades die on noise — and the least-bad 2024 and 2025. It earns under half as much in 2026, and sizing 2% against a full-range stop means much smaller positions.

The structure matters: EV keeps improving with RR all the way to 3.0, while win rate falls monotonically — 47.8% at RR 1 down to 28.9% at RR 3. Maximising expectancy would push RR higher. Two reasons not to. 2026 itself peaks at 2.0 and declines after, because a 3R target needs price to travel one and a half range-widths inside the hour and the hold cap truncates those attempts. And for a prop account, pass rate is not maximised by EV — see section 04.

RR 1.0 is clearly wrong. It buys a 47.8% win rate and gives up far more in edge: −0.133 against −0.024.

Answer: trigger at +0.5R, move to −0.5R. Never move to breakeven.

Breakeven is the single most destructive setting in this study. At a +0.25R trigger it turns +0.387R into −0.234R — it converts a profitable configuration into a losing one. A breakeven stop sits precisely where price routinely retests after a breakout: you get stopped for nothing on trades that would have worked, and you still keep every full loss.

Early triggers are worse than late ones at every destination. Moving the stop early tightens it while the trade is still inside noise.

On pure EV, not moving at all wins (+0.387). The move costs about 0.02R of expectancy. Keep it anyway.

The highest-EV configuration ranks eleventh. Turning the stop move off raises EV from +0.362 to +0.387 and lowers pass rate from 84.1% to 80.7%. The reason is in the sd(R) column: 1.37 against 1.49.

A prop challenge is a race between +8% and −10%, not a long-run expectancy game. Variance reduction is worth more than the expectancy it costs, because the drawdown barrier can end you before the edge has had time to express itself. Optimise for pass rate, not profit factor. They select different parameters.

Correction to the duration estimate

Earlier sections quote a median of 13 days to pass both phases. That assumed a setup every trading day. With a 15-minute cutoff only about 72% of days produce one, so the honest figure is ~18 trading days, roughly 3.6 calendar weeks.

06Roadmap

London and New York on gold

Session is already an input, so this is three runs of the same sweep with a different start hour — no code required.

Gold's peak volume window is the London–New York overlap, so on prior grounds London should give wider ranges relative to spread than Asia — which section 01 suggests is exactly where this strategy works best.

NDX100 — US100.cash

M1 history is already cached on the FTMO feed. Two things differ and both matter.

  • Cost structure. Index CFD spreads behave differently from spot gold. The stop-distance-versus-spread relationship has to be re-measured, not assumed.
  • Session meaning. NDX barely moves during Asia. Its range is defined by the 13:30 UTC cash open, and an Asia-session ORB on an index is close to meaningless. Expect New York to be the only viable run, possibly London for the European-hours gap.

Sizing needs no changes — lots derive from SYMBOL_TRADE_TICK_VALUE, so it is already contract-agnostic.

Why this helps the account pool

Six streams — three sessions across two symbols — trading the same day are far less correlated than one stream spread across six accounts by rotation. That delivers the decorrelation without the N× calendar penalty, and each account still takes one trade a day at 2% risk.

Testing six things at once

If six session/symbol combinations are each swept on 2026 data and the best is selected, the winner will look roughly one standard error better than it really is — about +0.05R here. On 119 setups per stream that is a real distortion.

The discipline that costs nothing: decide the threshold in advance — say 2026 EV above +0.20R with win rate above 40% — and trade every stream that clears it, rather than ranking and picking the top. Selecting a maximum from six noisy estimates is how a filter that works on paper fails live.

Known gaps, all of which flatter the simulation:

  • M1 OHLC rather than real ticks. Intrabar order is unknown, so when one bar contains both stop and target the simulation assumes the stop hit first. Real ticks still take out stops it misses.
  • Spread is a per-year median — 21 / 28 / 52 points — not the live value. The Asia open is thin and spreads spike there, so the median understates cost.
  • Commission modelled flat at $3.04 per lot per side, from the deal log.
  • 481 setups against MT5's 455. About 5% of days differ, most likely history gaps and entry-window boundary handling.

Treat absolute levels as ±0.05R. Rankings are reliable; levels are not. And 481 setups on one symbol and one session is enough to rank parameters, nowhere near enough to establish an edge.

08Bugs found and fixed

Three, all of which corrupted earlier results.

  • Take profit anchored to the range level, not the fill. RR=2 was delivering 1.24 realised, because entry happens past the level and the real risk was wider than the one the target was computed from.
  • Position size computed from the level too. Realised risk ran about 1.3× the configured percent on every trade; one loss reached 2.71× intended risk, and wins came out at 2.61R against an RR of 2.
  • The stop move ratcheted to breakeven. R was recomputed from the live stop each tick, so after the first move R shrank, the trigger re-fired, and the stop crept to entry. 47.5% of losses were landing at breakeven and 1.1% in the −0.5R band that was configured.

Fixing the third lifted win rates from 19/18/30% to 32/25/42% by year and took the strategy from −10.5R to −2.1R. It had also been masking the range-size effect, which is why an earlier pass through this data wrongly concluded there wasn't one.

Significant, and the direction holds in all three years. Filtering to the wide half turns 2024 from −0.060 to +0.101 and takes 2026 to +0.462.

But the test cheats

Ranking against the year's median uses information from the future — you cannot know 2026's median in January 2026. The implementable version is a rolling percentile against the last N sessions, which will perform worse, possibly much worse. +0.123R is an upper bound, not a forecast.

10Recommendations

  • Keep the current configuration. Midpoint stop, RR 2.0, stop move at +0.5R to −0.5R. Pass-rate optimum out of 546 candidates; no change warranted.
  • Do not chase the higher-EV variants. Turning the stop move off, or pushing RR to 3, raises expectancy and lowers pass rate. Wrong objective.
  • Never move the stop to breakeven. The one change here that can flip a profitable configuration to a losing one.
  • Build the rolling range filter and re-test. The only untested idea with a significant result behind it, and the difference between a 54% business and a 31% coin flip if the regime reverts.
  • Keep the 15-minute entry cutoff. It is a genuine peak — late breaks are net harmful.
  • Run London and New York next, then NDX100 on New York. Pre-commit the acceptance threshold before looking at results.
