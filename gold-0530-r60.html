01The rules

Everything is mechanical. No discretion, no judgement calls, and nothing that needs you awake at a screen.

  1. Mark the rangeTake the highest high and lowest low of the sixty M1 candles from 05:30 to 06:29 UTC. That box is the range.
  2. Split the box in half and see where the 06:29 candle closedClosed in the top half → you may only take an upward break. Closed in the bottom half → only a downward break. Breaks the other way are skipped — section 02.
  3. Watch for a break until 00:29The first M1 candle to close outside the box triggers the trade — above the high goes long, below the low goes short. Wicks poking through don't count. Nothing by 00:29 means no trade.
  4. Enter at marketOn the next candle, in the direction of the break.
  5. Stop at the range midpointHalfway between the high and the low. That distance is your 1R.
  6. Target at 3R3× the distance from entry to stop, measured from the actual fill.
  7. At +0.5R, move the stop to −0.5ROnce the trade is halfway to target, pull the stop so the worst case is half a loss instead of a full one. Once only — it never moves again.
  8. Flat after 180 minutesIf neither level is hit, close at market. 69% of trades finish on their own before this.
  9. Monday to Friday, one trade a dayOne position, then done regardless of the outcome.
  10. Risk 1.5% of the starting balanceFixed, not compounding. Position size = 1.5% ÷ the distance from entry to stop.

02The half-of-the-range rule

One filter, and it is the only one the edge needs. Draw a line across the middle of the range box and ask which half the 06:29 candle closed in. That half is the only direction you are allowed to trade today.

The whole rule

Closed in the top half → longs only. If price breaks down instead, you sit out.
Closed in the bottom half → shorts only. If price breaks up instead, you sit out.

Reading it live, in three seconds

  • At 06:30, look at the box and find its middle.
  • Did the last candle close above the middle? Only a break of the high counts today.
  • Below the middle? Only a break of the low counts.
  • Price breaks the wrong side → no trade, close the platform.
The one thing people get wrong

The candle's colour doesn't matter. A red candle sitting near the top of the box still counts as the top half — it drifted down a little but price is still up there. You are reading where it is, not which way it moved.

Why this filter is worth having

Trading every break returns +57.9 R from 147 trades at a 46.9% win rate. Trading only the correct half returns +61.0 R from 144 — virtually the same money from 3 fewer trades, at a 47.9 win rate. The 3 skipped trades contributed -3.1 R between them: all of the drawdown, none of the profit. Fewer trades at a higher win rate is exactly what a pass-rate account wants.

04Why each rule exists

Every number was measured, not chosen. Here is the reasoning in plain terms.

RuleWhy this and not something else
05:30 UTCNo market argument — this came out of an exhaustive search over every 30-minute open from 00:00 to 14:00 UTC. It is the Asia-to-Europe handover, and it is the only opening time whose result held in all three years.
60-minute rangeMeasured, and the opposite of what the Asia setup found. A 15-minute box at this hour gives EV +0.02 R; the hour-long box gives +0.34 R. Shorter is not universally better — it was better at 00:00 UTC.
Longs onlyThis is the weak point, not a feature. Allowing both directions drops EV from +0.34 R to +0.08 R, and shorts alone lose 0.17 R a trade. The filter is a bet that gold keeps rising, which it did across the whole test window.
3R target1R gives +0.06 R a trade here and 5R gives +0.24 R with a 34% win rate. 3R is the balance: 46% win rate at +0.34 R.
Stop at the midpointTested against 25%, 75%, 100% and 150% of the range, against ATR multiples and against fixed dollar stops. The midpoint won, but every rule within $3 of it scored the same — the distance matters, the formula does not.
No stop moveUnlike the Asia setup. Moving the stop to -0.5R at +0.5R cost 0.10 R a trade, because a 3R target needs room the tightened stop takes away.
180-minute capA 3R move from an hour-long box needs time. At 90 minutes EV falls to +0.19 R; at 60 minutes to +0.11 R.
Monday to FridayFriday is not the losing day at this hour that it is at 00:00 UTC. Dropping it removes 30 trades and lowers EV.
Cost sensitivity — read thisThe stop is narrow in the early years: a $2.80 median in 2024 against a $10.81 median in 2026. At $0.60 round-trip cost EV is +0.34 R; at $1.50 it is +0.10 R; at $2.00 it is negative. Slippage on stop exits is NOT modelled, and it bites hardest exactly where the stop is tightest.
Evidence strength — read this tooOf 1,534,680 combinations tested, 35,771 reached EV +0.20 R in 2026 alone and only 11 did so in all three years. Selecting on 2024-25 and then measuring 2026, 430 candidates averaged -0.11 R and just 5.3% still cleared +0.20 R — against a 2.48% base rate. This configuration is one of that 5.3%. Chance alone predicts about 23 such survivors.

A 6-loss run happened once, and at 1.5% risk it costs 9% — 3 points inside the 12% limit.

Realised drawdown reached 10.5% because a winner landed before that run started from a peak. The same sequence hitting a fresh account is the failure case, and it is the only one worth planning for — nearly every simulated failure is one long losing run rather than a slow bleed.

Position sizing, worked

  • Account $10,000, risk 1.5% → $150 per trade, every trade.
  • Range is 1,000 points ($10.00) → stop sits 500 points ($5.00) away.
  • Gold moves $1 per point per lot, so $5.00 of stop = $500 per lot.
  • Size = $150 ÷ $500 = 0.30 lots.

A wider range means a wider stop and therefore a smaller position — the dollar risk stays at $200 regardless. That is what makes every result comparable in R.
